*Circumstances do not define a man. What he does with them does.*

We are often quick to attribute a person's character, decisions and eventual outcomes to the circumstances they were born into. Their upbringing, financial background, environment or the people they grew up around become explanations for the person they turn out to be.

And while these factors undeniably influence us, I find it difficult to accept that they should define us.

---

We don't choose where we are born, the family we grow up in or the opportunities life initially affords us. Some are handed advantages, while others spend their lives trying to overcome disadvantages. Life is rarely fair in how it distributes its opportunities.

But consider this: if circumstances alone defined a person, wouldn't everyone raised in similar environments turn out to be the same? Yet they don't.

Two people can experience remarkably similar hardships and respond to them in entirely different ways. One may allow bitterness to consume them, while another chooses not to pass that bitterness on. One may use their circumstances to justify hurting others, while another makes a conscious effort to become someone different.

The circumstances may be similar. The choices are not.

---

This isn't to suggest that overcoming adversity is easy, or that everyone has the same capacity and opportunities to do so. Some circumstances are genuinely debilitating, and acknowledging that is important. But understanding someone's circumstances should not mean treating every subsequent action as inevitable.

Take addiction, for instance. Biology, environment, trauma and other factors can contribute to dependency. These deserve understanding and compassion. But compassion need not mean abandoning accountability. Understanding why someone finds themselves in a difficult situation and holding them responsible for their actions are not mutually exclusive.

The same applies to how we treat others. A difficult upbringing might explain someone's behaviour, but it doesn't automatically justify cruelty, betrayal or deliberate harm. Our past may explain parts of who we are, but it shouldn't become a permanent excuse for everything we choose to do.

---

I believe there is a distinction between what happens to us and what we choose to make of it. The former is not always within our control. The latter, although constrained by circumstances, is where our character begins to reveal itself.

Perhaps the real measure of a person isn't the hand they were dealt, but how they choose to play it.

> **Circumstances do not define a man. What he does with them does.**
